What to check before you share payment details with a client

Who it is for
freelancers starting with clients abroad
Time
10 minutes per new client
Last checked
25 November 2026

Most freelance scams are simple. They rely on you being excited and in a hurry. Run through this list before you send any payment details or start work.

Check the client is real

  • Look for a real website and a business email that matches it, not only a free email address
  • Search the person and company name on LinkedIn and Google
  • Ask for a short video call. Be careful with anyone who refuses
  • Search the company name with the word "scam" or "reviews"

Agree the work in writing

  • Write down what you will deliver, by when, and for how much
  • Agree how and when you are paid before you begin
  • For bigger jobs, ask for part payment first or split the work into milestones

What is safe to share

To get paid, a client may need your receiving account details. That is normal. Your password, your login codes and one-time passwords are never needed by anyone. Keep them to yourself.

Red flags

  • They ask you to pay a fee, buy equipment or send money before work starts
  • They send "too much" money and ask you to refund the difference
  • They ask you to move the chat to another app straight away
  • The offer pays far more than the work is worth
  • They pressure you to decide today
  • They ask for your login, codes or a remote-access screen share

Before you deliver the final work

  • Confirm the money has actually arrived in your account, not just that the client says it was sent
  • Keep copies of messages, agreements and payment confirmations
  • Start new clients with a small job first

If something feels wrong, stop and ask someone you trust. Losing a job is better than losing money.

What we are not sure about

This is general guidance, not legal advice. Scams change all the time, and we cannot cover every case. For a dispute or lost money, contact the payment provider and, where needed, a qualified person.
